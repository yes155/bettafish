## Evidence base

### Existing registered sources

- **SRC-021** — Betta husbandry and paternal behavior. Supports that male Betta splendens are bubble nesters; males build mucus-coated surface nests, retrieve fallen eggs and return eggs/larvae to the nest.
- **SRC-041** — Tank-size/furnishing welfare study. Nest building showed no statistically significant difference between tested housing conditions; the trend was highest in a small tank, which is useful evidence against treating nest presence as a simple happiness/welfare score.
- **SRC-054** — Evolutionary transitions between bubble nesting and mouthbrooding in Betta. Supports broader reproductive-strategy context without creating one habitat rule.

### Newly registered sources

- **SRC-072** — Bubble-nest mucus/pharyngeal-organ study. Supports glycoprotein-rich mucus and male anatomical specialization for bubble-nest construction.
- **SRC-073** — 2025 reproductive review. Supports bubble nesting and paternal-care context, while requiring caution when transferring captive breeding observations into pet-care rules.

## Key evidence conclusions

1. Bubble nesting is primarily a **reproductive/paternal behavior**, not a universal welfare test.
2. Male Betta splendens can build bubble nests without a female physically present.
3. The nest consists of air bubbles stabilized with mucus; male anatomy is specialized for producing this material.
4. During spawning, eggs and newly hatched larvae can be kept in or returned to the bubble nest by the male.
5. Nest presence alone does not establish that a fish is healthy, happy, ready to breed or kept under ideal conditions.
6. Nest absence alone does not establish illness or poor care.
7. Individual behavior and environmental context vary, so “no nest = problem” and “large nest = healthier fish” are unsupported simplifications.
