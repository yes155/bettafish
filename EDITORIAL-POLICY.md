# Editorial Policy Source

This repository source is rendered to `/editorial-policy/`.

BettaFish.website is being built as an independent educational resource. Content should help readers understand betta husbandry, aquarium conditions, behaviour and health while being candid about uncertainty and the limits of online guidance.

Credits link to permanent internal profiles. A reviewer credit may appear only after that page's review is recorded; assigning a reviewer in the content plan does not prove that an unfinished page has been reviewed.

## Evidence hierarchy

For welfare and health claims, editors should prefer:

1. peer-reviewed research;
2. aquatic veterinary and veterinary-school resources;
3. government, university extension and recognized animal-welfare guidance;
4. established aquarium references with named, experienced authors;
5. clearly labelled keeper observations when stronger evidence is unavailable.

Commercial pages, anonymous summaries and AI-generated answers are not primary evidence for high-risk claims.

## AI assistance

AI may assist with research organization, draft structure, code, QA and editing. It does not supply credentials, assume factual accountability or replace specialist review. Public claims must be traceable to reviewed sources, and the owner remains responsible for what is published.

## Health review

Disease, medication, prognosis and treatment pages remain blocked until their sources, claims and completed review record are verified. Robert Martinez, DVM, is the assigned clinical reviewer, but assignment is not approval. Kate Barrington's editorial review and Dr. Martinez's eventual content review are not presented as individualized diagnosis or a veterinarian-client-patient relationship.

## Corrections and updates

Substantive changes retain a review date and source trail. Corrections that alter safety advice, treatment guidance or a page's conclusion receive explicit review rather than a silent wording change.
